## 4.1 Research Question

How can a graph machine learning artifact be designed, implemented, and evaluated to improve the detection of money laundering networks in fintech transaction graphs, under conditions of class imbalance and limited labelled data — with design requirements derived from the operational context of financial intelligence units?

## 4.2 Search Strategy

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Database | Semantic Scholar |
| Date of search | June 2026 |
| Period covered | 2018–2025 |

**Boolean search string:**

(“graph” OR “graph machine learning” OR “graph neural networks” OR “GNN” OR "machine learning") 
AND 
(“money laundering”) 
AND 
(“fintech” OR “transactions” OR “blockchain”) 
AND 
("financial intelligence unit" OR "FIU")

## 4.3 Screening criteria

**Inclusion criteria**

- Studies applying GML, GNN, or other graph-based learning methods to anti-money laundering, financial fraud detection, or suspicious transaction identification

- Published between 2018 and 2025

- With empirical experiments on real or synthetic datasets (not purely theoretical proposals)

**Exclusion criteria**

- Studies applying classical ML without a graph component (standalone Random Forest, XGBoost, or MLP)

- Studies on fraud detection in non-financial contexts (healthcare fraud, electoral fraud, etc.)

- Studies using graphs only for visualization, not as a learning structure

- Publications prior to 2018

- Opinion pieces, editorials, or narrative reviews without systematic methodology

- Duplicates across databases
