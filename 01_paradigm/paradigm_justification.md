## 1. Topic and context

Money laundering is a complex financial crime that disguises illicitly obtained funds as legitimate assets. In response, public and private entities globally have designed Anti-Money Laundering (AML) detection systems to safeguard financial integrity and prevent these illicit activities.  With the recent explosion of Fintech startups in Latin America, and specifically in Peru, this represents a growing risk to the financial system that needs to be addressed through new detection methods based on a transaction graph modelling approach. 

## 2. Preliminary research question

Which design principles must a transaction graph modelling architecture satisfy to detect money laundering cases in the Peruvian Fintech environment?

## 3. Paradigm and justification

Design Science: The core of the work is building an artifact (a specialized graph-based model) and extracting generalizable design knowledge from its construction and evaluation through Hevner´s design science criteria. Pure positivism is rejected because the object of study does not exist until it is built; testing hypotheses against a pre-existing reality is not the contribution.

## 4. Methodological implications: data and methods

Data: (i) synthetic labelled data (such as Elliptic, AMLSim, among others); (ii) contextual documents (FATF typologies, Peruvian case law, criminology theories) for expert labelling and utility validation. Methods: Design Science. 

## 5. Maturity, tensions, and open doubts

Five key tensions persist: (a) data sensitivity versus reproducibility, tentatively addressed through a hybrid synthetic-plus-real-data strategy; (b) interpretability versus predictive performance, which frames explainability as a design constraint rather than an add-on; (c) whether an interpretivist paradigm should be incorporated alongside the dominant design science orientation; (d) venue fit, with technical outlets (NeurIPS, KDD, ICAIF) and policy-oriented venues still under consideration; and (e) the nature of the central contribution — a novel architecture, a methodological framework for FIUs, or both articulated as design knowledge.
